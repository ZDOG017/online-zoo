Did You Know?

Bald eagles can soar at altitudes of more than 10,000 feet and spot prey from over a mile away. Their incredible vision helps them locate fish, seabirds, and small mammals even in rough coastal weather.

Common name:
West End Bald Eagles
Scientific name:
Haliaeetus leucocephalus
Type:
Bird
Size:
2.5 to 3 feet body length
Diet:
Carnivore
Habitat:
Cliffs, forests, and shorelines near water
Range:
North America
View map

Bald eagles are powerful raptors known for their white heads, massive wingspans, and impressive fishing skills. They build some of the largest nests of any bird, often returning to the same site year after year and adding new branches each season. Though once threatened by habitat loss and pesticides, conservation efforts have helped many bald eagle populations recover. Watching the West End Bald Eagles in their coastal habitat offers a rare glimpse into their family life, hunting behavior, and the care they give to their young.
